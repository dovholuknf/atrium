## Test plan

## @LETTER@. Phone key bar

### @LETTER@1. A long press names the key
On a phone terminal hold any key on the bar for about half a second. A small bubble above it names it in words (enter, shift tab, escape, attach a file or photo) and stays inside the screen even for Esc and the paperclip. Release: the bubble goes and the key sent nothing. A quick tap still sends the key.

### @LETTER@2. ^C keeps its own rule
Hold ^C. The label shows at 400ms and the interrupt goes once at 600ms. A quick tap does nothing. Sliding the finger off any key before release cancels it and sends nothing.

### @LETTER@3. A tapped key is not left lit
Tap Left, Enter and the paperclip. Each returns to its resting look at once. Check the follow chip, the tray handle, the send button and the corner bell the same way. On a desktop the hover look is unchanged.

### @LETTER@4. Tapping another row sends nothing
Tap inside Claude Code's input on a row other than the cursor's. No keys are sent and a message queued to the session is not held by it. A tap on the cursor's own row still moves the cursor.

### @LETTER@5. The held chip names the right reason
Hover the `!` chip on a card. A line hold says to submit your text, a new-context hold says a new-context cycle is in progress and they go in after its wake prompt, and an unnamed hold reads about to be retried.
